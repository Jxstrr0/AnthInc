1. Why this batch

V0.2 plays well for the first two years and then plateaus: the money game ends at the Series A, and revenue is a faceless number from the serving slider. This batch gives the run a second act. A Series B that opens on a revenue milestone, and a customer era in which named enterprise accounts sign multi-year contracts, pay real money, and leave when the lab has an incident. The mix of investor money and customer money becomes the strategy it was always meant to be.

3. Systems

3.1 Series B (05_money.js)

  • Add K.rounds.b = { name: 'Series B', amount: 120e6, pctMin: 0.10, pctMax: 0.20 } and K.order = ['seed','a','b']. Everything else in the round machinery (offer, lapse, re-offer, lock) already works from data; check makeOffer holds the raise inside the bounds at B-era valuations and widen amount rather than the bounds if it clamps every seed.
  • The B milestone is always kind: 'revenue'. Set it when the A closes: value = sig2(max(K.ms.bRevMin, revenue × K.ms.bRevMult)), first pass bRevMin = 400000, bRevMult = 3, same 36-week window and warn cadence. A missed B milestone locks for 52 turns and comes back as a fresh revenue milestone (override msKinds for the B: revenue only).
  • B cash is the late-game compute lever: it should be enough to buy one year-3 cluster at the raised ceiling with runway to spare, and not enough to make accounts irrelevant. Tune with the bots.
  • Founder stake after seed + A + B typically 45–55%. Show it on the Boardroom panel as today.
  • Memo lines: "Series B opens if weekly revenue reaches $X by ." · "Series B offer: $120M for 15% at a $800M valuation. Expires ." · "Series B milestone missed…" — same voice as the A.

3.2 Enterprise accounts (new: 05_money.js owns the state, new file src/05b_accounts.js owns the logic)

New module so the edit surface stays one file. Pure, rng-only, same IIFE pattern. 05_money.step calls FR.accounts.step(s, rng, report) after revenue resolves.

State (add to plan/contracts.md §1):

accounts: {                          // 05b_accounts owns
  active: [ { id, name, sector, tier, pfPerWeek, feePerWeek, ends, signed, mood } ],
                                     //   tier 1..3; ends = last turn under contract; mood 0..100, drops on incidents
  offers: [ { id, name, sector, tier, pfPerWeek, feePerWeek, turns, minCap, minSafe, minTrust, expires } ],
  refreshAt,                         // turn the offer board refreshes (every 8 weeks)
  lost: [ { name, turn, why } ]      // last 10. why: 'churn' | 'expired' | 'dropped'
}
  • Unlock: the first offer appears when average cap ≥ 20 and public trust ≥ 45 (K.unlockCap, K.unlockTrust). One memo line: "Enterprise buyers are asking for meetings. First account offers on the Serving floor."
  • Offer board: 1–3 offers, refreshed every 8 weeks, at most K.maxActive (4 at start, 6 after the B closes) accounts live. Each names a fictional buyer (bank, retailer, insurer, logistics, government agency, pharma — a name generator with ~30 names, straight tone: "Halden Mutual", "Coastline Freight", "Province Health Authority"). Requirements: minCap, minSafe (every skill within N of cap) and minTrust; higher tiers ask more and pay more.
  • Contract: length 52 / 104 / 156 weeks by tier. pfPerWeek is reserved capacity: it comes out of the serving allocation before open-market demand (unserved contract PF breaches the contract; see churn). feePerWeek is a fixed fee, first pass tier × K.feeBase × (1 + 0.1 × year) with feeBase = 60000, paid on top of open-market revenue and not subject to the Zeta price drag (contracts are locked) but is subject to the incident revenue factor (a client under review pays half).
  • Signing: a small onboarding cost (K.signCost = 150000 × tier) and 2 weeks before revenue starts. Signing raises public trust +1 (tier 3: +2). Declining costs nothing.
  • Mood and churn: mood starts 70. Each week: +1 toward 80 if every skill's safe ≥ cap − 5 and the contract's PF was fully served; −3 for each week of unserved contract PF; −25 on any incident in the lab; −10 on a rival incident (sector-wide nerves). Mood < 30 → the account churns at the next turn with a memo and a news line ("Halden Mutual ends its contract with citing reliability concerns."). Churn costs trust −2. A final incident ends the run anyway; churn is the slow-burn version.
  • Expiry: at ends, the account renews automatically at the next tier's fee if mood ≥ 60, otherwise leaves quietly (why: 'expired').
  • Interaction with deals: DeepField's revenue share applies to account fees too (the share is on revenue, not on a channel).
  • Valuation: contracted annual fees count in M.valuation at K.revMultiple like other revenue; add a contracted-backlog term at a lower multiple (K.backlogMultiple = 4) so a full book lifts the B valuation.
